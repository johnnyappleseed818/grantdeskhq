### Factual frame

- Grants.gov describes post-award work as implementation, reporting, and closeout, including regular financial and programmatic reporting. Source: https://www.grants.gov/learn-grants/grants-101/the-grant-lifecycle
- Grants.gov says reporting requirements, schedules, and systems can vary by grant and directs recipients to review award terms and conditions. Source: https://www.grants.gov/learn-grants/grants-101/post-award-phase
- Do not convert those facts into a universal deadline, form, or compliance claim.

### Outline

1. Capture the agreement deliverable, reporting period, due date, owner, source system, and reviewer.
2. Map approved budget categories to reviewable accounting views and flag unmapped or ambiguous transactions.
3. Connect program evidence to the reporting period and preserve source links.
4. Assemble a review packet with variances, open questions, missing evidence, and approvals.
5. Set internal dates before funder due dates; escalate conflicting terms or incomplete information.

## Thursday candidate

Working title: Budget-to-actual grant reporting: how to build a reviewable evidence trail
Slug: budget-to-actual-evidence-trail
Audience: nonprofit finance teams, grants accountants, and grant managers
Search intent: source-linked financial reporting preparation
Draft status: SHADOW_DRAFT_REQUIRES_REVIEW

### Factual frame

- Grants.gov describes reporting information as including financial data, compliance information, and project data. Source: https://www.grants.gov/learn-grants/grant-reporting
- Grants.gov lists the SF-425 Federal Financial Report among standard reporting forms but does not make it universal for every award. Source: https://www.grants.gov/learn-grants/grant-reporting
- The forms repository identifies post-award reporting forms as reference material; recipients must follow their applicable submission process. Source: https://www.grants.gov/forms/forms-repository/post-award-reporting-forms

### Outline

1. Start with the approved budget and reporting period; preserve the exact source document.
2. Build a source-linked map from accounting exports to budget categories.
3. Separate confirmed amounts, pending classification, excluded costs, and unresolved items.
4. Pair financial explanations with program evidence only when the source supports the connection.
5. Create a reviewer packet with links, variance explanations, evidence gaps, and owner decisions.
6. Treat the funder portal/form and award conditions as the final submission authority.
