# 90-day post-award organic content plan

## Operating objective

Own the operational post-award reporting workflow: award → obligations → reporting plan → financial inputs → program inputs → evidence → report draft → human review → submission and closeout. Google Analytics 4 remains the traffic and acquisition source of truth; this plan does not create a duplicate analytics system.

**Publishing standard:** two original source-linked pages per week only when each clears usefulness, factual-grounding, differentiation, and conversion-path review. A reporting deadline, requirement, approval, or compliance assertion must trace to an appropriate primary source or be framed generally. General resources never replace award terms, funder instructions, accounting review, or human approval.

## AI-search and quality requirements

- Give each page a concise direct answer, clear definitions, practical steps, original checklist or table where useful, FAQs only when they add information, and visible source links.
- Use Article schema for individual articles and CollectionPage schema for the Resources index only. Never add review, rating, or FAQ schema that is not actually supported.
- Keep stable URLs, unique metadata, canonical tags, sitemap entries, descriptive internal links, and an intent-matched CTA.
- Use source-linked claims and visible uncertainty. Never manufacture funder requirements or treat general guidance as award-specific instruction.
- Link informational content primarily to Resources, How It Works, and Free First Report; use Pricing more directly only for commercial pages.

## Decision rules and measurement

Use GA4 to evaluate the path Resource landing → product CTA → signup → report started → checkout started → subscription started. Do not treat pageviews or email opens as primary success. Continue or expand a topic only after reviewing qualified signup, report-start, and activation signals; rework or retire weak topics instead of publishing volume for its own sake.

## SEO risks

No mass-generated pages, keyword stuffing, hidden text, thin templates, fabricated citations, copied competitor language, confusing comparison branding, or unsupported compliance claims. Do not publish drafts or placeholder resources.
