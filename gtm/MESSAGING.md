# GrantDeskHQ messaging system

## One-sentence promise

Turn your grant budget, accounting export, program updates, and funder form into a review-ready report draft—without replacing your accounting system.

## Short explanation

GrantDeskHQ uses AI to reduce the spreadsheet work between your accounting system and the funder's report. It suggests how transactions map to funder budget lines, prepares budget-versus-actual schedules, flags missing support, and drafts source-backed explanations for professional review.

## Why now

Nonprofit teams often have accurate accounting records and still assemble funder reports across Excel, Word, shared drives, and email. GrantDeskHQ is designed for that last-mile reporting workflow—not grant discovery, proposal writing, or bookkeeping.

## Customer value hierarchy

1. Reduce manual spreadsheet work.
2. Prepare funder-specific schedules and drafts from existing files.
3. Catch missing evidence and conflicting figures earlier.
4. Reduce repetitive follow-up between finance and program teams.
5. Keep a finance professional in control of every material mapping and statement.

## Message boundaries

Say:

- AI-assisted draft
- Suggested mapping

- Review-ready draft
- Missing-input checklist
- Professional approval required

Do not say:

- Fully automated
- Automated reconciliation
- Submission ready
- Audit ready
- AI compliance
- Guaranteed savings
- Error-free or hallucination-free

“Accurate books do not automatically produce the funder's report. Finance teams still have to map GL categories to the approved grant budget, calculate the funder's reporting period, collect program facts, explain variances, and transfer everything into a separate form. ### Post 2: AI trust

“An AI-generated grant report is not useful if the reviewer cannot see where each number or statement came from. Our standard is simple: material outputs need a source, calculated result, or confirmed response—and unsupported content stays blocked for review.”

### Post 3: fewer follow-ups

“Program teams should not receive a generic request for everything finance might need. The better workflow is to read the available evidence first, then ask only for the facts, receipts, and explanations that are actually missing.”
